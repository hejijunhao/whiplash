# Electronic genre conventions (research brief for Whiplash ontology v1)

Status: research only, compiled 14 Nov 2026 (PT) to resolve open questions in `docs/executing/ontology-v1-spec.md` and to size libraries for eight electronic genres. No hip-hop, garage, jungle, or breaks in this brief (those may already exist as plugin patterns; they are out of scope for this expansion).

Sources are production guides and beat dissections, not academic papers. Prefer Attack Magazine, Native Instruments, Splice, The Producer School, MusicRadar, Mix Elite, Maxim Schunk, Beatportal, TrackSensei, Melodigging, and SONAA. BeatKey and similar SEO production blogs are used only where they agree with those, and are marked.

Nothing here is hardware-verified on a Digitakt II.

## Shared electronic norms

- Almost everything here is 4/4 with a four-on-the-floor kick. Phrase lengths are 8, 16 or 32 bars; DJs count in those blocks, and a 24-bar section throws mixes off ([TrackSensei, techno](https://tracksensei.com/blog/how-to-make-techno); [Maxim Schunk, peak time](https://maximschunk.com/news/how-to-make-peak-time-techno)).
- Club records want 16–32 bar drum-led intros and outros so a DJ can mix ([BeatKey deep house](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-deep-house-music); [Mix Elite, tech house](https://mixelite.com/blog/how-to-make-a-tech-house-track-like-the-pros/); [BeatKey progressive](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-progressive-house-music)).
- House-family harmony favours minor keys and 7th/9th voicings over triads, mid-range clusters, roots left to the bass, and slow harmonic rhythm (one or two bars per chord) ([Violet Recording, house chords](https://violetrecording.com/house-chord-progressions/); [BeatKey deep house](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-deep-house-music)).
- "Repetitive" is genre-relative. Minimal and techno are built to hold a loop for a long time and evolve by automation or one element every 8–16 bars ([Maxim Schunk, minimal](https://maximschunk.com/news/minimal-techno-guide); [TPS, genre cuts](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter)). Progressive and tropical expect more obvious sectional change.

## 1. Deep house

**Signature:** 118–124 BPM (default 120–122), warm m7/m9 chords, swung off-beat hats, rolling bass, gradual 8–16 bar entries.

| Topic | Convention | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | 118–124 typical; classic 118–122, modern 120–124. 126 is already tech house. Default **122**. | [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter) (modal 122); [SONAA](https://sonaa.ca/en/styles/house/deep-house/) (118–123); [BeatKey](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-deep-house-music) (sweet spot 120) |
| Keys / modes | A, D, F, G, C minor; Dorian (im7–IV7) for the Larry Heard warmth. | [BeatKey](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-deep-house-music) |
| Progressions | **im7–IVm7** (Am7–Dm7), the two-chord foundation; **im7–IV7** Dorian soul (Am7–D7); **iim7–V7–Imaj7**; gospel **im7–bVImaj7–bVII7–i**; Afro **im7–bIIImaj7–bVII7–bVImaj7**; deep-tech one-chord **im9** held 4 bars. i–VI–III–VII (Am–F–C–G) is common house, slightly more "uplifting" than classic deep. Voicings: m7 minimum, m9/maj7 preferred; never bare triads. | [BeatKey](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-deep-house-music); [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/house-chord-progressions/) |

| Bass | Rolling, not stabs. Root with 5th and octave passing tones, light glide. Sustained more than tech house. | [BeatKey](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-deep-house-music); [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter); [SONAA](https://sonaa.ca/en/styles/house/deep-house/) |
| Arrangement | 6–8 min; intros/outros 16–32 drums-only; main loops 32; breakdown 16–32 with drums and bass out so chords breathe; add/remove something every 16 bars. | Repetition | 32 bars of the same two-chord vamp is normal if filter/perc evolve. Do not flag 16 unchanged bars as "too repetitive". | Same as arrangement sources |

## 2. House (classic / Chicago-ish)

**Signature:** 120–125 BPM, jacking 4/4, off-beat piano or organ stabs, octave-bounce bass, light or no swing.

| Topic | Convention | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | **120–125**. Attack uses 120–125; SONAA 120–124; MusicRadar tutorials at 120 or ~124. Default **123**. | [Attack, 80s Chicago](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/80s-style-chicago-house/); [SONAA, Chicago](https://sonaa.ca/en/styles/house/chicago-house/); [MusicRadar, classic house from scratch](https://www.musicradar.com/tutorials/music-production-tutorials/lets-go-back-to-the-mid-80s-where-dance-music-began-your-guide-to-creating-a-classic-house-track-from-scratch); [MusicRadar, piano chords](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/make-chicago-house-chords) |
| Keys / modes | Minor and major both common. A minor / C major relatives appear constantly. | [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/house-chord-progressions/) |
| Progressions | Piano-stab loops: **Em–G–F–Em** (MusicRadar / Move Your Body family); **C–Bb–Cm–F** (often revoiced toward C–Bb11–Abmaj7–F); **i–VI–III–VII** (Am–F–C–G); **i7–iv7** vamp; Cm7–Gm7 with a last-bar Em7 twist. Stabs are short, often avoiding the kick downbeat, mid-range, rootless or tight. | [MusicRadar from scratch](https://www.musicradar.com/tutorials/music-production-tutorials/lets-go-back-to-the-mid-80s-where-dance-music-began-your-guide-to-creating-a-classic-house-track-from-scratch); [MusicRadar piano](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/make-chicago-house-chords); [Attack, chord stabs](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/passing-notes/levelling-up-your-chord-stabs/); [Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/house-chord-progressions/) |
| Drums | 909/808/707 language. Kick 4/4, sometimes a last-offbeat kick or mid-bar ghost. Clap/snare on 2 and 4; a late or "jacked" clap (nudged a 16th, or a clap on the last division of the bar) is the Chicago feel. Closed hats 16ths with velocity variation, or 8ths; open hat every offbeat. Syncopated rim or cowbell. Swing **50–55%** or straight; groove often comes from the late clap, not heavy shuffle. Fills: simple tom rolls every 8 bars. | [Attack, 80s Chicago](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/80s-style-chicago-house/); [SONAA, Chicago](https://sonaa.ca/en/styles/house/chicago-house/) |
| Bass | Short monophonic saw, filtered; **octave bounce** between root and off-beat is the Chicago signature. | [SONAA, Chicago](https://sonaa.ca/en/styles/house/chicago-house/) |
| Arrangement | Jacking repetition is the point; 4–5 drum elements, not a dense bus. Fills every 8. Breakdowns exist but are less "sacred" than progressive. | [SONAA, Chicago](https://sonaa.ca/en/styles/house/chicago-house/) |
| Repetition | High tolerance. A stab loop can run a long time if velocity/rhythm of stabs varies. | Same |

## 3. Tech house

**Signature:** 124–128 BPM (default 126), busy 16th hats, short bouncy off-beat bass, change one thing every 8 bars, DJ-tool intro/drop/outro.

| Topic | Convention | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | **124–128**, modal **126**. Slower becomes deep house; faster leans techno. | [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter); [Mix Elite](https://mixelite.com/blog/how-to-make-a-tech-house-track-like-the-pros/); [Abletonic](https://www.abletonic.eu/en/blog/how-to-make-tech-house-ableton); [EDMProd / Fisher](https://www.edmprod.com/how-to-make-tech-house/) (125–128, sweet 126) |
| Keys / modes | Minor, bass-friendly (F, F#, G minor often cited). Phrygian used when a darker line is wanted. Harmony is secondary to groove. | [EDMProd](https://www.edmprod.com/how-to-make-tech-house/) |
| Progressions | Sparse: one or two notes for bars at a time; optional dark one- or two-chord loops; vocal chops as hook rather than long progressions. | [Mix Elite](https://mixelite.com/blog/how-to-make-a-tech-house-track-like-the-pros/); [Abletonic](https://www.abletonic.eu/en/blog/how-to-make-tech-house-ableton) |
| Drums | Dense (5–7 elements): punchy short kick 4/4; clap/snare on 2 and 4, often layered; **closed hats on every 16th**; open hats on offbeats; shaker/tick; woodblock or rim accent. Hits just before the beat for a disco push. Swing/shuffle on in-between notes (Mix Elite "about 40%" on FL's knob — treat as mild shuffle, not deep-house 55–60%). Velocity variation is mandatory. | [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter); [Mix Elite](https://mixelite.com/blog/how-to-make-a-tech-house-track-like-the-pros/) |
| Bass | Short, bouncy, repetitive. One or two pitches, mostly off-beat or 16ths around the off-beat, rests between notes. Rhythm does the talking. | [Mix Elite](https://mixelite.com/blog/how-to-make-a-tech-house-track-like-the-pros/); [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter) |
| Arrangement | 8-bar grid. Typical: 32 intro, 16 break/build, 32 drop, 16 break, 8 build, 32 drop 2, 32 outro (~5–7 min). Every 8 bars, add, drop or vary one element. Mute kick for the last bar of a build. | [Mix Elite](https://mixelite.com/blog/how-to-make-a-tech-house-track-like-the-pros/); [Abletonic](https://www.abletonic.eu/en/blog/how-to-make-tech-house-ableton); [The Ghost Production](https://theghostproduction.com/make-tech-house-without-weak-drums-mud/) |
| Repetition | A 1-bar bass can carry a record, but the drum/FX layering must move every 8 bars. Flag a static 16-bar full groove more readily than deep house. | Same |

## 4. Progressive house

**Signature:** 122–128 BPM (default 124), long filtered builds, lush 7th/9th pads, 16–32 bar drumless breakdowns, patient journeys.

| Topic | Convention | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | **122–128**. Classic/Pryda 122–126; festival 126–128; melodic prog 122–124. Sweet spot **124**. | [BeatKey progressive](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-progressive-house-music); [Myloops](https://www.myloops.net/arranging-a-progressive-house-track) (120–128) |
| Keys / modes | A minor and D minor most cited; 7ths, 9ths, sus4 — not triads. | [BeatKey progressive](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-progressive-house-music) |
| Progressions | **Am–Dm–G–C** (i–iv–VII–III), circular; **Am–F–C–G** (i–VI–III–VII) as restrained uplift; two-chord **Am7–Dm7** for 64 bars while texture builds; Prydz-like **Am–Am/G–Fmaj7–E7** (descending bass); darker **Am–Bb–Am–E7** (Phrygian bII). Pads with long releases; stabs in the groove, pads in the break. | [BeatKey progressive](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-progressive-house-music) |
| Drums | Functional, not busy: 4/4 kick, clap 2 and 4, closed hats on 8ths, open hats offbeat, sparse syncopated perc, optional rim on 3. Groove is often the sidechain pump. | [BeatKey progressive](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-progressive-house-music) |
| Bass | Warm, root-following, not aggressive. Filter on the lead is "the performance". | [BeatKey progressive](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-progressive-house-music) |
| Arrangement | Journey, not a sudden drop. Intro 16–32 drums only; build 16–32 adding a layer every 8; groove 16–32; **breakdown 16–32 with kick and bass gone**; build 8–16; peak 16–32 with one new element; cooldown; outro 16–32. Filter opens over 16–32 bars, not 4. Last beat before a drop often nearly empty. | [BeatKey progressive](https://beatkey.app/how-to-make-progressive-house-music); [We Make Dance Music](https://www.wemakedancemusic.com/en/news/progressive-house-arrangement-guide-daw); [Myloops](https://www.myloops.net/arranging-a-progressive-house-track) |
| Repetition | Harmony may sit still; if filter/layers do not move for 16 bars, that is a problem. Count automation as "change". | Same |

## 5. Techno (club / peak-time, not hard techno)

**Signature:** 128–134 BPM (default 130), kick-led, little or no chordal movement, off-beat or rolling bass, plateau arrangement in 16/32-bar blocks.

| Topic | Convention | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | Club **128–135**; peak-time often 130–134; hypnotic/deeper 126–130. Production guides still centre ~128–132; charts have drifted faster, but hard techno is out of scope. Default **130**. | [TrackSensei](https://tracksensei.com/blog/how-to-make-techno); [Maxim Schunk, peak time](https://maximschunk.com/news/how-to-make-peak-time-techno); [Beatportal, peak time](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/783088-step-by-step-guide-to-producing-techno-peak-time-driving-in-the-style-of-layton-giordani-eli-brown-and-adam-beyer) (126–132, start 128) |
| Keys / modes | Sparse: Aeolian loops or **Phrygian** (E–F or Am–Bb) for darkness. Often one minor chord or two-chord loop for 16–64 bars. | [Armon, techno](https://armonai.app/en/genres/techno); [Zynthion, techno progressions](https://www.zynthion.com/tools/chord-progressions/techno) (lighter source; use only as a typological check) |
| Progressions | Prefer **one chord / pedal**, or a two-chord dark vamp (i–bII, i–VI). Avoid lush four-chord "house" loops as the default techno bed. Stabs: m7, sus2, or a 5th. | [Brizm, minimal techno](https://brizm.dev/learn/minimal-techno/) (also applies to sparse techno harmony); Armon as above |
| Drums | Punchy short kick every beat, tuned. Off-beat open hat; closed 16ths optional; clap/snare on 2 and 4 more common in peak-time than in minimal. Rolling percussion between kicks. | [Maxim Schunk, peak time](https://maximschunk.com/news/how-to-make-peak-time-techno); [Beatportal](https://www.beatportal.com/articles/783088-step-by-step-guide-to-producing-techno-peak-time-driving-in-the-style-of-layton-giordani-eli-brown-and-adam-beyer) |
| Bass | Off-beat or rolling short notes, one or two pitches, ducked under the kick, mono low end. | [Maxim Schunk, peak time](https://maximschunk.com/news/how-to-make-peak-time-techno) |
| Arrangement | Plateaus, not EDM build-and-drop. 32-bar drum intro (no bass/hook); 16–32 build; 32 main; 16–32 breakdown (**kick drop** is the main tension tool); 32 second main with one new element; 32 outro. All changes on 8/16/32. One or two peaks only. | [TrackSensei](https://tracksensei.com/blog/how-to-make-techno); [Maxim Schunk, peak time](https://maximschunk.com/news/how-to-make-peak-time-techno) |
| Repetition | High. 32 unchanged bars is not a fault if the kick/bass lock; flag busyness more than repetition. | Same |

## 6. Minimal (minimal techno and minimal house)

**Signature:** 124–130 BPM, very sparse drums, space as an instrument, microtiming and one-element-per-phrase evolution, little or no song harmony.

Treat as **one genre with two tempo/feel presets**: `minimal.house` (~126, percussion-led) and `minimal.techno` (~127–130, kick-led), sharing the same sparse grammar.

| Topic | Convention | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | Minimal house **124–128**, modal 126; minimal techno **124–130**, modal 127 (some guides 125–132). Default **126** for the house preset, **128** for the techno preset. | [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter); [Attack, minimal house](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/minimal-house/) (123–127, swing 55–60%); [Maxim Schunk, minimal techno](https://maximschunk.com/news/minimal-techno-guide); [Brizm](https://brizm.dev/learn/minimal-techno/) (120–130) |
| Keys / modes | Often none. When present: a single stab (m7, sus2, 5th), a filtered pluck, or a one-note acid line. | [Brizm](https://brizm.dev/learn/minimal-techno/) |
| Progressions | Default: no progression, or a one-chord pedal. Do not paste deep-house four-chord loops here. | [Brizm](https://brizm.dev/learn/minimal-techno/); [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter) |
| Drums | 3–4 elements. Kick 4/4; soft conga/shaker offbeats; sparse rim on 2 and 4 (not always a full snare); sometimes only an off-beat open hat. Claps used sparingly, sometimes every second bar. Quiet 16th ghosts and **swing 55–60%** (Attack) or small shuffle (Schunk). Polymeter (3-step perc) is a minimal-techno colour. | [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter); [Attack, minimal house](https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/minimal-house/); [Brizm](https://brizm.dev/learn/minimal-techno/); [Maxim Schunk, minimal](https://maximschunk.com/news/minimal-techno-guide) |
| Bass | Minimal house: **stabs**, one or two short notes per bar, space between them. Minimal techno: short notes, often tonic and octave, classic rolling off-beat bass. A constant rolling 16th bass under sparse drums is tech house, not minimal house. | [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter); [Brizm](https://brizm.dev/learn/minimal-techno/) |
| Arrangement | 7–10 minutes common. 16–32 bar drum intros/outros. Almost no verse/chorus; change one element every 8–16 bars; automation (filter, send, pan) is the arrangement. | [TPS](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/the-producers-guide-to-tech-house-vs-deep-house-vs-minimal-the-differences-that-actually-matter); [Maxim Schunk, minimal](https://maximschunk.com/news/minimal-techno-guide); [Brizm](https://brizm.dev/learn/minimal-techno/) |
| Repetition | Highest tolerance. 32–64 bars of the same skeleton is normal. Flag only true stasis (no automation, no entry/exit) or sudden density that breaks "minimal". | Same |

## 7. Tropical house

**Signature:** 100–115 BPM (default 108), major-key pop harmony, soft 4/4, shakers and mallets, hooks instead of heavy drops.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | **100–115**. Splice: keep 100–115; Melodigging: typically 100–115. Default **108** (middle of the Kygo-ish 100–110 pocket). This is the one genre clearly below the house 120 floor. | [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/tips-to-for-producing-tropical-house/); [Melodigging](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/tropical-house) |
| Keys / modes | Major or warm modal mixture. Pentatonic toplines. | [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/tips-to-for-producing-tropical-house/); [Melodigging](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/tropical-house) |
| Progressions | **I–V–vi–IV** and **vi–IV–I–V** (C–G–Am–F / Am–F–C–G). maj7/9 for a brighter pad. This is pop-house, not a deep minor vamp. | [Melodigging](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/tropical-house) |
| Drums | Soft 4/4 kick, clap/snare on 2 and 4, off-beat hats, shakers, bongos/congas, light swing. Transients smooth, not punchy. | [Melodigging](https://www.melodigging.com/genre/tropical-house); [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/tips-to-for-producing-tropical-house/) |

## 8. Melodic techno

**Signature:** 120–128 BPM (default 124), cinematic minor/modal chords every two bars, rolling bass, atmospheric pads, long filtered intros and emotional breakdowns.

| Topic | Convention | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | **120–130** (NI); **120–128** (Soundtrap, "faster than deep house, often slower than traditional techno"). Tutorials often start at **122**. Default **124**. | [Native Instruments](https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/melodic-techno); [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/melodic-techno/) |
| Keys / modes | Minor or modal; rich, cinematic voicings (add9, maj7, sus2) with smooth inversions. | [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/melodic-techno/); [NI](https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/melodic-techno) |
| Progressions | Chords often change **every two bars**. i–VI–III–VII (Am–F–C–G) with add9/maj7/sus2 voicings is widely used; other emotional minor loops in the same family. Leads: short stutter notes, pitched, following the chords. | [NI](https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/melodic-techno) (every two bars; rolling bass on the root, then moving); Soundtrap (minor/modal) |
| Drums | Stripped but tight: 4/4 kick, clap 2 and 4, detailed hats (often busy 16ths for momentum), shaker, sparse rim/noise. Less dense than peak-time techno, more driving than deep house. | [NI](https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/melodic-techno); [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/melodic-techno/) |
| Bass | Rolling 16ths or off-beat rolls on the chord root, with pitch moving when the chord moves; optional accent note. Sidechain to the kick. | [NI](https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/melodic-techno) |
| Arrangement | Gradual, Afterlife-like: long filtered kick-and-bass intro; sparse breakdown with pads; preview the drop filtered; a moment of space; full drop with rolling bass and lead. Second drop adds a layer; stripped outro. | [NI](https://www.native-instruments.com/blogs/blog/melodic-techno); [Soundtrap](https://blog.soundtrap.com/melodic-techno/); TPS Afterlife-style tutorials (MRAK, Massano) as supporting arrangement colour |
| Repetition | Medium. Harmony can loop, but the lead/filter should move; 16–32 bars without a layer or filter change is stale. | Same |

## Cross-cutting implications for the open questions

These are research inferences for the conversation with the implementer, not yet decisions.

- **Deep house progressions** should not be "i–VI–III–VII only". That loop is house-wide and slightly uplifting. The deep-house core is **im7–IVm7**, the **Dorian im7–IV7**, gospel and Afro variants, and a one-chord m9. Keep i–VI–III–VII in the library, tagged as uplift.
- **Microtiming and ghosts** are not optional flavour: every house/minimal guide treats swing, late claps, and velocity ghosts as the groove. Defaults should include them; Phil's ears only confirm Digitakt audibility.
- **Octave-pump bass** is a Chicago-house signature, not a universal default. Use it in `house`, not as the deep-house or techno default.
- **Breakdown behaviour is genre-specific:** progressive/deep/melodic often remove kick and bass; techno's main tool is the kick drop; tech house removes or filters the low end; tropical reduces drums and keeps the hook; minimal barely "breaks" at all, it subtracts one element.
- **Repetition thresholds must be per-genre.** 16 unchanged bars is fine (even short) for minimal and techno, stale for tropical, and for progressive is stale only if texture is also static.
- **Filter as energy:** core to progressive and important in melodic techno; not the default meaning of "energy" in tech house or Chicago house (those change layers and bass). Keep filter moves opt-in, on by default only in progressive (and maybe melodic techno) forms.
- **Existing plugin genres** (`soul`, `breaks` in `patterns.py`) can stay in the plugin; this brief does not ask to delete them. The eight genres above are the ontology v1 set. Soul already overlaps classic house slightly; do not rename it, just don't treat soul/breaks as this expansion's focus.
